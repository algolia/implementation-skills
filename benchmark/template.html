    Focused Algolia tests show examples of better advice and more complete repairs, alongside ties and losses. Results vary by task, model and whether the guidance is loaded. They do not yet establish a consistent overall advantage.

    November 2026 · Current 22-skill library

    Eighteen paired comparisons (36 runs) tested GPT-6.1 Terra, GPT-6.1 Sol and Claude Sonnet 5.5 with and without the library. Models and versions were fixed within each comparison. Advice used supplied evidence; backend audits used a live service; browser tests used a simulated service. Their different scoring scales are kept separate.

    TaskSkills higherTieBare higherScale

    Bare means the same model without the skill library. Each lane has six paired comparisons: two per model. These are small, selected ecommerce samples, not a measure of customer business impact.

    The single raw browser advantage needs context: a product modal blocked the grader’s next background cart click. A separate replay closed the modal and received a correctly attributed conversion; the original score remains unchanged. Browser tests used a simulated service, not actual API acceptance.

    Backend examples and skill activation

    Scores out of 10 (bare → skills available): Terra scored 7 → 9, then 7 → 7 on its reset pair. Sol scored 8 → 9 on its reset pair and 9 → 9 on the other pair. Claude scored 9 → 7 and 8 → 7, but neither skills-available run loaded a skill. This is an activation gap in this setup, not evidence that skill content caused the lower scores.

    Observed explicit reads or invocations occurred in 5 of 6 merchandising, 4 of 6 backend and 1 of 6 frontend skills-available runs. A read is not proof of influence, and an absent read does not prove descriptions had no effect.

    Further validation of isolated candidates

    A separate v3 decision batch of eight pairs did not meet its predefined criteria for a consistent advantage, with ties, losses and reviewer disagreement. A v4 artifact-repair batch of eight pairs produced eight ties: both arms scored 19–20/20 and every run scored 10/10 on mechanical checks. Both arms were near the scoring ceiling on the repair tasks. These tests did not demonstrate a consistent quality advantage or broad equivalence.

    Methodology

    How to interpret the tests

    The results are useful signals for these tasks, with clear limits on what they establish.

    Study scope and activation

    The samples cover selected ecommerce tasks and are small. In September, the skills arm also had the Algolia CLI, so it was not an isolated test of skills. Modes differed: some tasks relied on discovery and others explicitly prompted or mandated skills. November tested library availability; the v4 candidate repair batch explicitly invoked the skill. Availability did not guarantee invocation.

    Additional November study limitations
    Scoring and outcomes

    Model-based reviewers scored quality, with one excluded run graded by the controller. Live checks measure a separate set of outcomes. No human comparison arm or measured business impact was included; these results do not establish ROI or guarantee production readiness. Reported time and tokens are run-level measurements, not a complete invoice.
